OSCILLARIS is a text-command RPG that takes place inside an oscilloscope. Type your way through its wireframe streets, talk to the programs who live there and use your words to defeat rogue signals. The game wanted to be found. Now it's up to you.

Something was here before you

OSCILLARIS is built on the ruins of its predecessor. Its programs don't remember what came before, but the world does, if you know where to look.

Features

  • Type commands to walk, examine and interact with the world.
  • Explore the wireframe world and find secrets within its glowing vector lines.
  • Meet programs with their own personalities and stories to unfold.
  • Survive incoming signals with their own agendas using your spelling skills and letter play.
  • Discover fragments of a past that remembers more about you than you do.

Not every signal is friendly

An echo remains from the past, running somewhere in the code. Explore the BOOT sector, but don't go looking for something you won't want to find.

The RNG Machine

Godot · Solo developer · June 2026

A roguelite dice-rolling card battler. Roll dice, drag them onto enemy cards and bank your Overkill to unlock lasting upgrades.

  • Roguelite
  • Systems design
  • GDScript
Play on itch.io
Released
7 June 2026
Engine

Role

Music

The RNG Machine is a roguelite dice-rolling card battler. Each run, you roll dice and drag them onto enemy cards to deal damage. Beat an enemy by more than its health and you bank the difference as Overkill, a currency you spend to unlock new abilities and upgrades.

Features

  • Dice-based combat: drag and assign dice to enemies, matching numbers or symbols for powerful effects like Fire, Ice, Lightning and Chain.
  • Overkill economy: every point of excess damage is banked and kept between runs.
  • Persistent skill tree: spend Overkill between runs to unlock lasting upgrades across six branches.
  • Varied enemies: from pesky regular cards to unique bosses.

Kick Ass Kook

Godot · Solo developer · In development

A fast-paced parkour platformer built around one idea: keep moving. Wall kick, slide and launch across a minimalist pixel city.

  • Platformer
  • Movement design
  • Game feel
Status
Prototype · Release TBD
Engine
Godot
Role
Solo developer
Genre
Parkour platformer

Don't stop. Don't slow down. Don't look down.

Kick Ass Kook is a fast-paced parkour platformer built around one idea: keep moving. You play as the Kook, tearing through a semi-open pixel city where every rooftop, wall and rail is a route waiting to be found.

Wall kick from building to building and build speed with every link in the chain. Hit a slope and you're sliding whether you like it or not, so time your jump to launch off it with everything you've built up. Cans, boxes and other junk in your way? Kick them aside, or come down hard enough to smash straight through.

Push your luck too far, though, and a big drop will leave the Kook in a heap on the pavement. The city rewards flow, not recklessness. Learn its lines, chain your moves and find out just how fast the Kook can really go.

Features

  • Momentum-driven parkour: run, skid, jump, wall kick and slide. Every move feeds into the next.
  • Kick everything: wall kicks for climbing, and kicks to clear cans, boxes and anything else that slows you down.
  • Slopes that launch you: slide down, jump off and carry that speed through the air.
  • Risk vs. reward: fall too far and you'll collapse, so find the routes that keep your flow alive.
  • A semi-open world: explore a crisp, minimalist 8-bit city full of shortcuts, rooftops and hidden lines.
  • Reactive feel: snappy controls, screen shake and effects make every landing and kick hit hard.

Flick Of Da Wrist

Godot · Solo developer · May 2026 · Unreleased

A velocity-driven speedrun prototype: keep your speed up, roll through tight gaps and race the clock. The early prototype that became Kick Ass Kook.

  • Speedrun
  • Level design
  • UI/UX
Created
27 May 2026
Status
Unreleased
Engine
Godot
Role
Solo developer

How fast can you go? As fast as you can keep it up.

Flick Of Da Wrist was an early prototype for what is now Kick Ass Kook. It was a speedrun game built almost entirely around velocity, pushing players to the extreme. There was no top speed: as long as you kept your momentum going, you could go as fast as you wanted.

Rolling was the core move. A well-timed roll let you squeeze through small gaps without losing any of the speed you'd built up, so the fastest routes came from chaining runs, slopes and rolls together. A timer ticked away in the corner and every level saved your personal best, giving you a reason to go back and shave off another few hundredths of a second.

Every level had two ways to finish. You could simply reach the goal, or you could collect every coin. Grabbing all the coins was the harder challenge, but it offered a faster way to win if you could pull it off.

Features

  • Uncapped momentum: keep your speed up and there's no limit to how fast you can go.
  • Rolling: slip through small gaps while keeping all your speed.
  • Two ways to win: reach the goal, or take the riskier, faster route of collecting every coin.
  • Speedrun tools: a live timer, personal bests per level, a level select, plus Editor and Custom modes.

What I learnt

It taught me a lot about level design, as well as UI/UX: building menus, the timer and tracking systems in Godot using Control nodes. Those lessons fed straight into Kick Ass Kook.

Cilloscope

Godot · Solo developer · April 2026 · Unreleased

A vector-line space sandbox. Fly your ship, walk around its interior, EVA on a tether to make repairs and take over other ships in a semi-open system of planets and asteroids.

  • Vector graphics
  • Systems design
  • GDScript
Created
9 April 2026
Status
Unreleased
Engine
Godot
Role
Solo developer

Cilloscope was my testing ground for drawing everything with vector lines instead of the sprites I'd normally make. Every ship, station, planet and asteroid is built from glowing green lines, like a picture traced on an old oscilloscope screen.

You pilot a spaceship through a semi-open stretch of space filled with planets and asteroid belts. The ship isn't just a vehicle, though: mid-flight you can leave the controls and walk around its interior, using the different stations that keep it running. When something breaks outside, you suit up and go on an EVA, drifting out on a tether to repair the hull by hand. Spot another ship out there and you can board it and take control.

Features

  • Vector-line visuals: everything drawn in code as glowing lines rather than sprites.
  • Walkable ship interiors: move around your ship during flight and interact with its systems.
  • Tethered EVAs: head outside on a tether to make repairs.
  • Ship takeovers: take control of other ships you find.
  • Semi-open space: planets and asteroid belts to explore.

What I learnt

I learnt a lot during development, especially about writing clean code and organising systems and resource files so a project can grow without falling apart. While I think I could have done better, it was a major step forward for my programming knowledge.
